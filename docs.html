Astra Terms

Thank you for using Astra! We're happy you're here. Please read this Terms of Service agreement carefully before accessing or using Astra. Because it is such an important contract between us and our users, we have tried to make it as clear as possible. For your convenience, we have presented these terms in a short non-binding summary followed by the full legal terms.

Account Responsibility

Each individual may maintain only one primary account for rated competitions. Sharing credentials or participating in a rated round from multiple accounts is strictly prohibited and may result in rating revocation or account suspension.

Contest Integrity & Fair Play

During an active contest, all submitted code must be written solely by you. You may use pre-written personal templates published prior to the start of the round, provided you follow our core fair-play rules:

  • No external collaboration: Discussing problem statements, hints, or solutions with anyone before the contest ends is forbidden.
  • No AI code generation: Using automated code-generation tools during live rated rounds is considered a violation of contest rules.
  • No judge abuse: Deliberately attempting to crash, probe, or overload the evaluation servers will result in immediate suspension.

Public Submissions & Licensing

Once a contest has concluded, submissions made to public problemset archives become viewable by other learners for educational purposes. Problem authors retain full credit for their original statements and test data.

Astra Privacy Policies

Your privacy matters to us. This policy explains what information Astra collects when you register, submit solutions, and compete in rated rounds, as well as how we safeguard your data and respect your control over your account.

Information We Collect

We collect only the minimum information required to operate an online judge and maintain accurate rankings:

  • Account profile: Your chosen handle, email address, and optional profile biography.
  • Submission records: Source code, compiler choice, execution time, memory usage, and judge verdict.
  • Interface preferences: Local display settings such as light or dark theme stored in your browser via localStorage.

How Your Data Is Used

Your data is used exclusively to evaluate code submissions, compute contest standings and Elo rating changes, display public leaderboards, and send essential account notifications. We never sell or rent user data to third parties.

Data Retention & Deletion

You may request account deactivation or permanent data deletion at any time through our Report an issue page.

/ Security Policies

Astra Security Policies

Executing untrusted code safely is at the core of Astra's engineering. This document outlines how our evaluation workers isolate submissions, how user accounts are protected, and how security researchers can report vulnerabilities.

Isolated Judge Sandboxing

Every user submission is compiled and executed inside an isolated Linux container with restricted system calls, zero external network access, read-only root filesystems, and strict CPU time and memory quotas.

Account & Transport Protection

All account credentials are salted and hashed using modern cryptographic algorithms, and all traffic between your browser and Astra is encrypted over HTTPS.

Responsible Vulnerability Disclosure

If you discover a security issue in Astra's web application or judge workers, please report it privately via our Report an issue form or email hello@astra.dev so our team can patch it promptly.

Astra Glossary

A quick reference for judge verdicts, contest formats, and competitive programming terms used across Astra.

AC — Accepted

Your program produced the correct output on all hidden test cases within the time and memory limits.

WA — Wrong Answer

Your program terminated normally, but its output did not match the expected answer on at least one test case.

TLE — Time Limit Exceeded

Your program took longer to execute than the maximum CPU time allowed for the problem.

MLE — Memory Limit Exceeded

Your program allocated more memory than the problem's permitted limit (typically 256 MB).

RE — Runtime Error

Your program exited with a non-zero status due to an unhandled exception, segmentation fault, or division by zero.

Upsolving

Practicing and solving problems from a contest after the live round has concluded.

Astra Policy FAQs

Quick answers to common questions regarding contest rules, code visibility, and platform policies. For general platform questions, visit our full FAQ page.

Can I use open-source algorithm templates during a contest?

Yes, as long as the template or data-structure library was publicly available prior to the start of the contest and was not written specifically for any problem in the active round.

Are my submissions visible to other users?

During a live contest, your source code remains private until the round ends. In the public Problemset and Gym archives, accepted solutions can be viewed by other users to support learning.

How do I appeal a verdict or report a broken test case?

Submit a ticket on our Report an issue page with the problem code, submission ID, and counterexample input so our problem setters can investigate.
